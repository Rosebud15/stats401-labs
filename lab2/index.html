Lab 2: Multivariate Visualization with D3

This visualization compares city population, average temperature, development level, and region.

Bar length shows population. Circle position shows temperature, circle size shows development level, and bar color shows region.

Design Justification

This visualization uses several visual channels to represent the four required variables. Population is shown by the length of each horizontal bar, making it easy to compare city sizes. Average temperature is represented by the horizontal position of the white circle, using the temperature scale at the top of the chart. Development level is shown by circle size, with larger circles representing higher development levels. Region is represented by bar color so that cities from the same region can be identified quickly.

The cities are also ordered by development level from High to Medium to Low. Within each development level, cities are sorted from cooler to warmer temperatures. This creates more meaningful groups and makes patterns easier to notice. For example, with this sorting method, it is very easy to notice that cities with colder average tempearture tend to have higher development levels, and more developed cities tend to have a larger population. Overall, the visualization allows viewers to compare population, temperature, development level, and region while also seeing how these characteristics vary across cities.
