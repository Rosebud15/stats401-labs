Lab 6: GDP Hierarchy with Two Treemaps

Both treemaps show the hierarchy Continent → Area → Country. Rectangle area represents GDP in billions of U.S. dollars, while color represents GDP status.

GDP Status Legend

Treemap 1: Squarify

Squarify favors compact rectangles, which makes country areas easier to compare.

Treemap 2: Slice-Dice

Slice-Dice alternates horizontal and vertical subdivisions to make hierarchy levels visually explicit.

Design Description

I use rectangle area to encode GDP because area is the defining quantitative channel of a treemap and makes part-to-whole relationships visible within each region. GDP status is categorical, so I encode it with three distinct hues: blue for Increase, neutral gray for Unchanged, and orange for Decrease. This avoids implying an ordered numeric scale while keeping the categories distinguishable. Continent and area labels occupy reserved header space inside their parent rectangles, and borders reinforce the Continent → Area → Country nesting without requiring extra color channels. Country names appear only when a rectangle is large enough to support readable text; every country remains accessible through the tooltip.
I chose Squarify and Slice-Dice because they emphasize different aspects of the hierarchical data. Squarify attempts to create compact rectangles with relatively balanced aspect ratios, which makes rectangle area, and therefore GDP, easier to compare across countries. Slice-Dice instead alternates the subdivision direction at each level of the hierarchy, making the Continent → Area → Country structure easier to follow visually. I did not use Slice or Dice alone because they tend to produce long, narrow rectangles in a single orientation, which can reduce label readability and make area comparison more difficult. Binary also produces effective treemaps, but its visual organization is less distinctly different from Squarify. Using Squarify and Slice-Dice therefore provides a clearer comparison between quantitative readability and hierarchical readability.
